Your hardware, on the network.

Keep your device in your laboratory and let people run experiments on it. A small agent on the computer next to the hardware connects it to vonNeuro Messenger. You describe what the device can do, set its limits, and decide who may use it; Messenger handles accounts, queues, metering and results.

Early access. The network runs today, with our bench as its first node. Any account can register a node from My hardware; while the hub is on its temporary tunnel, we approve each new node before it pairs. Write to us if you want to talk it through first.
No public address

The agent dials out to the hub over HTTPS. Nothing listens on your network, and no port is opened.

Your limits win

Your driver checks every job before anything is driven. The hub can tighten what reaches you, never loosen it.

You stay in charge

New devices start private. Share with everyone, only with people you grant time to, or nobody; switch off or disconnect at any time.

How it works

  1. Describe the device in a driver. A short Python class lists your devices and the operations they offer, each with typed parameters and their ranges (a voltage up to 1 V, a channel from 0 to 15). Its check() refuses anything unsafe; its run() drives the hardware and writes the result files.
  2. Register and pair the computer. Sign in to Messenger, open My hardware, register the node (for now we approve each one), and get a one-time pairing code. The agent turns it into the node's own secret. You own the node, and can add colleagues as its admins; you stay an ordinary user of everyone else's hardware.
  3. Run the agent. Your devices appear on the network with their operations. People run them from the website, the Python lab or their own Python, and you see who used what, for how long and with how much energy.

On the computer next to the hardware

pip install https://series-marina-gigantic.ngrok-free.dev/downloads/vonneuro-node.zip   # Python 3.11+
vonneuro-node pair --hub https://series-marina-gigantic.ngrok-free.dev --code ABCD-EFGH
vonneuro-node check          # validates your driver's operations, offline
vonneuro-node run            # connects; your devices go online
vonneuro-node service install   # optional: start it at logon

The first run uses a simulated RC circuit, so you can try the whole loop before touching your hardware.

A driver

import json
from vonneuro_node import Driver, DeviceSpec, Operation, RunResult
import my_lab.probe_station as instrument          # your existing control code

class CrossbarDriver(Driver):
    def devices(self):
        return [DeviceSpec(slug="crossbar-8x8", name="8×8 memristor crossbar", kind="memristor",
            specs={"cells": 64, "max_voltage_v": 1.0},
            operations=[Operation("read", "Read one cell at a small voltage",
                params={"row": {"type": "integer", "minimum": 0, "maximum": 7},
                        "col": {"type": "integer", "minimum": 0, "maximum": 7},
                        "voltage": {"type": "number", "exclusiveMinimum": 0, "maximum": 0.3,
                                    "unit": "V", "default": 0.1}},
                result="read.json")])]

    def check(self, slug, operation, params, ctx):
        return None if instrument.ready() else "The probe station is busy"

    def run(self, slug, operation, params, ctx):
        current = instrument.read(params["row"], params["col"], params["voltage"])
        (ctx.output_dir / "read.json").write_text(json.dumps({"current_a": current}))
        return RunResult(exit_code=0, usage_seconds=0.2)

Set [driver] class = "your_module:CrossbarDriver" in node.toml. The agent keeps a local journal of every job it ran, so you have your own record.

Two example drivers come with the agent: drivers/simulated.py, the shortest one to start from, and drivers/bench.py, the driver of our own bench. The vonNeuro bench is not a special case: it is a node like yours, paired the same way, with an RC board and a memristor array behind multiplexers and its safety rules written in its driver.

Early-access terms, in plain words

  • You keep your hardware and your data. The device stays yours and in your laboratory; you can switch it off or disconnect the node at any time, and its jobs stop.
  • You are responsible for its safety. Your driver's checks are the last word on what reaches the hardware. Messenger checks parameters against what you declare, but cannot know your device.
  • What the hub keeps. Accounts, job records (who ran which operation, with which parameters, how long), and the result files your driver returns, for as long as you choose on the device (forever, or 1 to 365 days).
  • The directory. A device is listed publicly only after we review it; we may unlist or switch off a device that misbehaves or is reported, and tell its owner.
  • Early access. The network is new and offered as is, without guarantees of availability. Hardware time is free for now; any change to that will be announced before it applies.
